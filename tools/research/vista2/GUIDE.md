Lateral commands must be physically executable. A minimum-jerk lateral move of displacement D metres over duration T seconds demands peak lateral acceleration 5.7735*D/T^2. Its available budget is the lesser of the tyre limit scaled by local grip and the steering-geometry limit v^2*tan(maxSteer)/wheelbase. At low speed the steering limit binds hard: in the measured unmarked-dirt-lot failure, a command at 3.15 m/s demanded 2.52 times the geometry ceiling. On ice, the tyre ceiling for a passenger car is only about 1.47 m/s^2. Slow vehicles and icy roads therefore need long, gentle lane_offset and change_lane manoeuvres. Never overlap a lateral manoeuvre with speed(stop) on the same actor: a stationary body cannot translate sideways. Measured failure: a stop at t=3.0 removed steering authority from a lateral reference that still ran to t=3.66.

Environment is authorable and simulated, so stop reducing weather and road-surface briefs to a bare dry-corridor mechanism. The exact static invocations are `{"action":"set_world","key":"env.weather","value":"snow"}`, `{"action":"set_world","key":"env.timeOfDay","value":"dusk"}`, and `{"action":"set_world","key":"env.frictionScale","value":0.35}`. Weather accepts clear, cloudy, overcast, light_rain, heavy_rain, wet_road, fog_light, fog_dense, snow, sleet; timeOfDay accepts dawn, morning, noon, afternoon, dusk, night, night_lit. Weather sets a scene friction scale (light_rain 0.78, wet_road 0.72, heavy_rain 0.58, sleet 0.42, snow 0.35) and the engine applies it per actor per tick. Add a bounded low-grip region with `{"action":"set_world","key":"env.surfacePatch","value":{"id":"ice-zone","kind":"ice","atM":45,"lengthM":35,"laneOffsets":[0]}}`; repeat the action with a unique id for another patch. Kinds and grip are ice 0.15, packed_snow 0.30, spilled_oil 0.25, wet_leaves 0.45, standing_water 0.50, sand 0.50, loose_gravel 0.60, polished_asphalt 0.75, grit_treated 1.15. A patch only matters if it covers the ego's braking zone BEFORE the conflict; a patch behind the conflict point is decoration and will not certify.

Low grip is a hard physical constraint, not flavour. Grip scales both braking and cornering through the same friction circle, so on ice a passenger car has roughly 1.5 m/s^2 of braking and about 1.47 m/s^2 of lateral authority. The frozen gate rejects any collision and the evaluator rejects a trace whose required deceleration exceeds the grip-scaled ceiling as physically unavoidable. A low-grip scenario authored at ordinary speeds with a late reaction therefore fails twice over: it either makes contact or becomes unavoidable. Author low grip with correspondingly lower speeds, longer gaps and earlier onsets, and give lateral moves long durations.

Set painted-line appearance only after selecting a site, with one contiguous reference-lane approach window such as `{"action":"set_world","key":"env.marking","value":{"id":"faded-zone","quality":"faded","atM":50,"lengthM":30,"laneOffsets":[0]}}`; quality is crisp, faded, absent or misaligned. The harness proves that the window covers at least 20 m of the ego's driven, camera-framed approach and lowers to one non-junction lane. Junction lanes deliberately carry no painted boundaries and are rejected rather than silently treated; scattered fragments are also rejected, so do not split one treatment into many ids. Measured failure: a snow-covered-markings episode authored 100 m of absent markings across 8 fragments, but four fragments were junction lanes and four were disconnected 2-to-26-point stubs nowhere near the camera; all 240 rendered frames contained zero treated boundary segments, and the semantic oracle correctly rejected it. This is appearance only: lane geometry stays authoritative and marking quality provably does not change vehicle dynamics, so use it to make a marking-degradation brief visible, never to explain a trajectory.

Set wind exactly with `{"action":"set_world","key":"env.wind","value":{"directionDeg":90,"speedMps":0,"gust":{"startS":3,"durationS":4,"peakSpeedMps":35}}}`. `directionDeg` is the direction the air travels TOWARD, counter-clockwise from corridor-forward, so +90 pushes a forward-travelling actor left. Wind is a real aerodynamic side force scaled by each class's projected side area, and correcting it consumes friction-circle budget. It is only VISIBLE on high-sided bodies: measured, a 25 m/s gust moved a truck 0.19 m, which is under two pixels in the evidence and cannot be judged, while a box truck or bus at about 18 m/s road speed with a 35 m/s peak sustained 3-4 s deviates 0.79-0.97 m, which is legible. Centre the gust on the approach and conflict; a gust that peaks long before the conflict is not the phenomenon.

Discrete non-kinematic actor state is authorable through `verb: "set"` interactions and is now DRAWN in the 2D evidence, so it can finally be judged. The exact keys and values are `pose.gesture` (none, wave_through, halt, point, phone; vehicles may use everything except phone, so a driver can wave), `pose.paddle` (stowed, stop, slow), `pose.stopArm` (retracted, extending, extended), `lights.indicator` (off, left, right, hazard), `lights.brake` (boolean), `lights.emergency` (off, flashing, flashing_siren). The catalog has `pedestrian.traffic_marshal` for a director, plus school bus, police, fire and ambulance vehicles. Critically, a gesture is only an EXPLANATION: it must coincide with the motion it explains. A halt must land while traffic actually stops, and a wave_through while the waving vehicle actually holds and the ego actually proceeds. A gesture with no matching kinematics is unprovable and will be rejected, correctly.

Author only what the 2D evidence can show, because that schematic footage is the sole acceptance authority. It now draws lanes, junctions and crosswalks from the real map, actor bodies by class, motion trails, authored props (cones, barrels, barricades, arrow boards, signs), sight-line occluders, static stop/yield/speed signs, live traffic-signal heads including flashing and dark, surface patches labelled with their material, faded, absent, misaligned and obscured markings, all the discrete actor states above with short text tags, and a line stating weather, time of day and grip. It does NOT show photometrics: glare, dazzle, exposure and fine visual texture cannot be proven, so a brief that depends on them will not certify no matter how correct the simulation is.
